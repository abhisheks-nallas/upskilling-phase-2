# Performance Ratings and Promotions

## Rating scale
Reviews use a five-point scale. A rating of 1 means needs improvement, 2 means partially meets expectations, 3 means meets expectations, 4 means exceeds expectations and 5 means outstanding. Most people are rated 3, which is a good and normal rating. A calibration panel makes sure ratings are comparable across teams, and no more than 10 percent of a team can receive a 5 in one cycle.

## Writing the self-assessment
The self-assessment is due 7 working days before your review meeting. Write down your main deliverables, what you learned, where you helped others and what you want to do next. Use numbers and links where you can, such as a feature shipped, a defect rate or a talk given. Managers read the self-assessment before writing theirs, so a short and specific note works better than a long one.

## Peer feedback
You nominate two peers to give feedback and your manager can replace one of them if there is a conflict of interest. Peers answer a short form and the answers are shared with you without names. Peer requests go out three weeks before the review and peers have 10 days to reply. Giving thoughtful feedback to colleagues who ask is part of your own job.

## Promotion eligibility
To be considered for promotion you need at least 12 months at your current level and a rating of 4 or higher in each of your last two cycles, or one rating of 5. Your manager nominates you with a short case showing the work you already do at the next level. Being eligible does not guarantee a promotion, because the panel also looks at the open roles and the business need.

## Increments and timing
Promotions and salary revisions are decided once a year after the October cycle and take effect from 1 January. An increment in a promotion year is usually larger than the normal increment. Employees confirmed after 1 October are considered in the next cycle.

## If you disagree with a rating
Talk to your manager first, and ask for the reasons in writing. If you still disagree, raise it with your HR business partner within 7 days of the review meeting. The calibration panel looks at the case again and the decision after that review is final for the cycle.
